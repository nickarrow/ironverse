Sunlight streams through the window of my room, awakening me from depths of my exhausted slumber. I blink away the blurriness of my vision and rub the sleep from my eyes. A emerge from my bed with a yawn, stretching swinging my legs over the side of the bed setting to work pulling on my pants and boots. 

As I stand I catch a glimpse of my reflection in the bowl of water on the table nearby and can't help but be caught a little off guard by the face that greets me. My wild, bushy beard is gone, a tasteful mustache and goatee taking its place. My hair is not a tangled, matted mess, been trimmed to a medium length then brushed and cleaned. I look presentable, like a man with something to lose. It feels *good*.

Smiling to myself, I pull on my new clothes and am pleased to find they fit very well. Fane, my new tailor, did an excellent job making them fit like a glove. I test my range of movement and am rewarded with no tightness or chafing. I feel like a king even if I only look like a well-off dandy.
I can't ignore how out of place my broken blade looks amongst this ensemble, nor how primitive my improvised dagger looks. I make a mental note to find a smith who can repair my blade, or sell me a suitable alternative. I'm not entirely sure what to do with the spider fang dagger, but it's a worthy trophy if nothing else.

My contemplation and reflection is interrupted by a soft rap at the door. Gregory, the innkeepers voice calls out
"Master Stone, are you decent?"

I take a quick glance at my drastically improved reflection before I reply
"Never better, come on in!"

The door swings open with a creak and the innkeeper shuffles in with a small tray of fruits and fresh bread buns. When his eyes meet mine his face brightens as he proclaims
"Well well well, you're a gentleman after all! Quite the stark contrast from the flotsam that came stumbling into my inn yesterday."

I nod, somewhat ashamed of my first impression but too grateful for his hospitality to deny his assertion. "All the credit belongs to Portia, she's the one who helped me find this refined man under all that wild hair."

The innkeeper chuckled and nodded in turn. "That girl has a habit of bringing out the best in the people around her. She's my pride and joy, even more so than this inn."

I grab a grape from the platter and pop it into my mouth as he talks, it's an explosion of juicy flavor so early in the morning. I fear at this rate any food I find on the road from here on out will be wholly inadequate by comparison. I snap back to the conversation.

"Indeed, she's a fine host. I can see why she was so busy last night when I came in, she must have many regulars who visit for her company."

The old man shot me a wry look
"Yes, I have had the occasional regular who has needed reminding of the employee/customer relationship boundaries. Thankfully most smarten up once sober and apologize. Those who don't are often shown the door by my other regulars. They're all rather protective of her as it turns out."

I raise my hands in from of myself in a disarming gesture
"I have no intentions of causing trouble, I assure you. You've been extremely accommodating and I find myself as charmed by your inn as I am your daughter."

The old man laughs, a proper, full throated bellow and claps me on the back with surprising force for his frame.
"Ha! Good answer. She was late to return from visiting you last night, she told me the two of you had a good chat and she reckons you might find work as a bardic storyteller if you need coin.

I inwardly wonder if that was something I had ever done before, in the forgotten times before I washed ashore.

He mistakes my downcast expression for embarrassment and nudges me with his elbow and adds in a knowing tone.
"Cheer up lad, that's a good sign. Truth be told, she's always had the freedom to court who she likes, she's just never had much interest in the folks around here."

I look up in alarm and begin to correct him when he interrupts me.
"Don't treat me like a fool, it's nothing to be ashamed of."

I fall silent, not wishing to insult him. Besides, he wasn't exactly wrong: Portia was quite fetching, even if I was in no shape to court her.

After a moment of silence, the innkeeper stands smiles apologetically and gestures at the platter on the table I had momentarily forgotten about.
"Forgive me, I didn't come up here to put my nose where it doesn't belong. Breakfast is served, even if it is a few hours past when I normally offer it. Eat up and regain your strength. When you're done eating, come downstairs and let's talk. I think I may have a job for you if you're looking for work."

"Thank you, I may do just that."

---

I ate my fill, sating a hunger I didn't realize was so strong until I had cleared the platter of food. Not the for the first time today, I feel a strong gratitude for the small comforts of this place.

Pushing aside the platter, I take a moment to consider my options.
I could get work from Gregory, whatever that might entail it could bring me some income while I sort out the rest of what I'm doing. 

My eyes are drawn to the cylindrical case that rests on the table beside my bed. I could also investigate the odd codex, though I doubt I'll find someone who knows how to interpret it easily and even if I could I doubt I would be able to trust anyone who could with something so important or valuable it's the only thing I wasn't wearing that made it ashore with me.

I absentmindedly find my hand resting on the hilt of my broken sword.
That settles it for me: I need to get my blade fixed, then I can both focus on the codex and protect it from any interested parties.
